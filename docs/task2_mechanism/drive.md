# 2a - drive choice

I would use **4 wheel skid steer**. Its not the most efficient when turning because the wheels scrub a bit, but it is simple and all four wheels can keep contact with the floor. That should help on the ramp and speed breakers.

| option | ramp | breakers | tight turns | stable with arm | cost / setup | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 wheel + caster | 3 | 2 | 5 | 2 | 5 | 3.35 |
| 4 wheel skid steer | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3.85 |
| mecanum / omni | 2 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2.75 |
| tracks | 5 | 5 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 3.75 |

Scores are 1 to 5. Weights used: ramp 25%, breakers 20%, tight turns 20%, stability 20%, cost/setup 15%. Weighted total is out of 5.

Runner up is tracks. They have good grip but the setup is more complicated and turning in the 250 mm track could be awkward.

## rough layout
- chassis plate: 240 x 180 mm
- wheelbase: 170 mm
- track width: 150 mm
- wheel diameter: 70 mm in the model

The wheels are compact, but 70 mm diameter means radius is only 35 mm. That does not guarantee it can climb a sharp 40 mm step. If the breakers are close to vertical, 90 mm wheels are a safer starting point, but the whole size needs checking again.
